## 2. Story-Fakten (belegt, mit Humor-Potenzial)

### F1 – Der Kaiser und sein Hahn namens „Rom“ (410)
Laut Prokop erfuhr Kaiser Honorius in Ravenna, „Rom sei untergegangen“, und dachte an seinen Hahn namens Rom.
> “At that time they say that the Emperor Honorius in Ravenna received the message from one of the eunuchs, evidently a keeper of the poultry, that Rome had perished. And he cried out and said, "And yet it has just eaten from my hands!" For he had a very large cock, Rome by name; and the eunuch comprehending his words said that it was the city of Rome which had perished at the hands of Alaric, and the emperor with a sigh of relief answered quickly: "But I, my good fellow, thought that my fowl Rome had perished." So great, they say, was the folly with which this emperor was possessed.”

Quelle: Prokop, Vandalenkrieg III.2 – https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/16765
**Einschränkung:** Prokop schreibt selbst „they say“ (Hörensagen, ~140 Jahre später). Im Video als
„legend says“ / „according to one historian“ erzählen, nicht als gesicherte Tatsache. Laut Text ein **Hahn** („cock“, „fowl“).

### F2 – Das Römische Reich wird versteigert (193)
Nach dem Mord an Kaiser Pertinax bieten Sulpicianus (im Lager) und Didius Julianus (vor dem Tor) um die Prätorianer.
> “Then ensued a most disgraceful business and one unworthy of Rome. For, just as if it had been in some market or auction-room, both the City and its entire empire were auctioned off. The sellers were the ones who had slain their emperor, and the would‑be buyers were Sulpicianus and Julianus, who vied to outbid each other, one from the inside, the other from the outside. They gradually raised their bids up to twenty thousand sesterces per soldier.”

> “Some of the soldiers would carry word to Julianus, "Sulpicianus offers so much; how much more do you make it?" And to Sulpicianus in turn, "Julianus promises so much; how much do you raise him?"”

> “had not Julianus raised his bid no longer by a small amount but by five thousand at one time, both shouting it in a loud voice and also indicating the amount with his fingers.”

Quelle: Cassius Dio 74.11.2–5 – https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/74*.html
Herleitung Gebot: 20.000 + 5.000 = **25.000 Sesterzen pro Soldat**. Die Historia Augusta bestätigt die Summe und sagt, er habe sogar mehr gezahlt:
> “Although he had promised five and twenty thousand sesterces to each soldier, he gave thirty.”

Quelle: Historia Augusta, Didius Julianus 3.2 – https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Didius_Julianus*.html
(Thayer-Anmerkung dort: “According to Herodian ( II .7.1) Didius did not pay what he had promised, because the money was not available.” → Zahlung umstritten; im Video nur „bid 25,000 per soldier“.)
**Nicht bestätigt:** Umrechnungen wie „10 Jahresgehälter“ oder eine Gesamtsumme (Zahl der Prätorianer unbelegt) – nicht verwenden.

### F3 – Kaufreue: 66 Tage Kaiser
> “And so it came about that Julianus was slain as he was reclining in the palace itself; his only words were, "But what evil have I done? Whom have I killed?" He had lived sixty years, four months, and the same number of days, out of which he had reigned sixty-six days.”

Quelle: Cassius Dio 74.17.5 – https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/74*.html
(Historia Augusta: “He ruled two months and five days.” – leicht abweichend; Dio = Zeitzeuge → **66 Tage** verwenden.)

### F4 – Sein Vorgänger hielt 87 Tage

Quelle: Cassius Dio 74.10.3 – https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/74*.html

Quelle: Historia Augusta, Didius Julianus 4.3–4 – https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Historia_Augusta/Didius_Julianus*.html

### F6 – Jahr der fünf Kaiser (193) und „sechs Herrscher in wenigen Monaten“ (238)
> “His death would mark the end of the second emperor in the “Year of the Five Emperors.””

Quelle: WHE, Didius Julianus – https://www.worldhistory.org/Didius_Julianus/
Die fünf (Herleitung aus WHE Septimius Severus): Pertinax, Didius Julianus und die drei Rivalen

### F7 – Kaufreue Teil 2: Die Garde wird aufgelöst

### F8 – Der letzte Westkaiser trug die Namen von Stadtgründer UND erstem Kaiser – als Verkleinerung
> “the appellations of the two great founders, of the city and of the monarchy, were thus strangely united in the last of their successors. The son of Orestes assumed and disgraced the names of Romulus Augustus; but the first was corrupted into Momyllus, by the Greeks, and the second has been changed by the Latins into the contemptible diminutive Augustulus.”

Quelle: Gibbon, Kap. 36 – https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25717
Primärquelle zur Verkleinerungsform:
> “the power in the West was held by Augustus, whom the Romans used to call by the diminutive name Augustulus because he took over the empire while still a lad, his father Orestes, a man of the greatest discretion, administering it as regent for him.”

Quelle: Prokop, Gotenkrieg V.1 – https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/20298
Herleitung: „-ulus“ = Verkleinerung → „Augustulus“ ≈ „little Augustus“ („Mini-Augustus“).
Thayer-Anmerkung (Excerpta Valesiana, Anm. 9): “His title Augustus was changed to Augustulus in mockery of, or perhaps because of, his youth.”
**Nicht bestätigt:** ein genaues Alter (oft „ca. 16“) – keine wörtliche Quelle gefunden; im Video „a teenager“/„just a kid“ nur mit Prokops „still a lad“ abgedeckt.

Quelle: Excerpta Valesiana 8.38 – https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Excerpta_Valesiana/2*.html

### F10 – Odoaker schickt die Kaiserinsignien nach Konstantinopel („Ein Kaiser reicht“)
> “They solemnly “disclaim the necessity, or even the wish, of continuing any longer the Imperial succession in Italy; since, in their opinion, the majesty of a sole monarch is sufficient to pervade and protect, at the same time, both the East and the West.”
> “and he gratefully accepted the Imperial ensigns, the sacred ornaments of the throne and palace, which the Barbarian was not unwilling to remove from the sight of the people.”

Quelle: Gibbon, Kap. 36 – https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25717 ; Gibbons Anm. 124 nennt als Quelle den Zeitgenossen Malchus: “Malchus, whose loss excites our regret, has preserved (in Excerpt. Legat. p. 93) this extraordinary embassy from the senate to Zeno.”

**Einschränkung:** Malchus selbst konnte nicht im Wortlaut eingesehen werden; Insignien-Detail über Gibbon (Sekundärquelle) belegt.

### F11 – Alarichs „Trojanisches Pferd“: 300 Jungs als Geschenk (410)

## 3. Mythen & Klischees – was die Quellen sagen

### M1 – „Rom ist an einem Tag gefallen“ / „476 war das große Ende“
- Datum selbst unsicher: Gibbon, Anm. 125: “The precise year in which the Western empire was extinguished, is not positively ascertained.” (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25717)
- Das Gebilde lief weiter: “c. 476 CE is the traditionally accepted date for the end of the Western Roman Empire, that entity did continue on under the rule of Odoacer” (WHE, https://www.worldhistory.org/Western_Roman_Empire/) [korrigiert Faktencheck: „c.“ ergänzt – der Satz lautet im Original „While c. 476 CE is …“]
- Der Prozess zog sich über Generationen: 395 Teilung → 410 → 455 → 476 (Zeitleiste oben); Ostrom bis 1453.
- Gag-Idee: „Rome wasn't built in a day – and it didn't fall in one either.“ (Sprichwort, kein Faktenanspruch.)

### M2 – „Es gab EINE Ursache“ → in der Forschung umstritten
> “Although historians generally agree on the year of the fall, 476 CE, and its consequences for western civilization, they often disagree on its causes.”
> “Whatever the cause, whether it was religion, external attack, or the internal decay of the city itself, the debate continues to the present day”

Quelle: WHE, https://www.worldhistory.org/article/835/fall-of-the-western-roman-empire/ [korrigiert Faktencheck: URL leitet per 301 hierhin um]
Gibbon (1776–89) als klassische, heute nur eine Stimme: “But the decline of Rome was the natural and inevitable effect of immoderate greatness.” … “instead of inquiring why the Roman empire was destroyed, we should rather be surprised that it had subsisted so long.” (Kap. 38, „General Observations“, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25717)
→ Im Video: Ursachen nie als gesichert darstellen („historians still argue“).

### M3 – „Bleivergiftung hat Rom zerstört“
- Belegt ist nur: Römer kannten die Gefahr selbst. Vitruv: “Water conducted through earthen pipes is more wholesome than that through lead; indeed that conveyed in lead must be injurious, because from it white lead is obtained, and this is said to be injurious to the human system.” (Vitruv 8.6.10 – **[korrigiert Faktencheck]** Überschrift „Chapter 6“ steht auf der Seite, Abschnitt „10.“; https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Vitruvius/8*.html)
- Dass die Forschung die Blei-These als Hauptursache verwirft, ist **nicht bestätigt** (keine erreichbare Fachquelle mit Wortlaut). → Im Video Blei höchstens als „one wild theory“ / Mythos-Gag, nie als Ursache.

### M4 – „Die Barbaren haben Roms Monumente aus Hass zerstört“
> “Our fancy may create, or adopt, a pleasing romance, that the Goths and Vandals sallied from Scandinavia, ardent to avenge the flight of Odin … But in simple truth, the northern conquerors were neither sufficiently savage, nor sufficiently refined, to entertain such aspiring ideas of destruction and revenge.” – Gibbon, Kap. 71, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25717

### M5 – „Vandalen = Vandalismus“
- Belegt: “The name "vandal" has now become synonymous with careless destruction owing to the accounts by Roman writers” (WHE, https://www.worldhistory.org/Vandals/).
- **Nicht bestätigt:** die verbreitete Angabe, das Wort „vandalisme“ sei 1794 von Abbé Grégoire geprägt worden (Britannica gesperrt). Nicht verwenden.

### M6 – „Die Kaiserzeit war ständiges Chaos“
Gegenbeleg Gibbon (Kap. 3) für die Zeit Augustus–Commodus: “the two centuries from Augustus to Commodus passed away unstained with civil blood, and undisturbed by revolutions.” (https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/25717) – Das Chaos (193, 238) kam später. Gibbons Urteil ist übertrieben (er nennt selbst 4 Kaiser in 18 Monaten 68/69); nur als Kontrast nutzen.
